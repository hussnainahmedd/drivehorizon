# Existing implementation and verification history

This note records the completed gameplay work that was present when the
DriveHorizon completion pass began. It is retained as project history and is
not a claim of native rendering certification.

## Existing gameplay

The game provides free-roam driving in a locally generated coastal city. The
vehicle uses fixed-step force-based bicycle dynamics with acceleration,
braking, reverse, steering, handbrake, collisions, damage, fuel, visual
suspension and reset recovery. Three camera modes, mouse orbit, zoom,
look-around, headlights, horn and synthesized engine/road/impact audio are
implemented.

Traffic contains fourteen route-following vehicles with signal, corner,
following and player-avoidance behavior. The world includes roads,
intersections, buildings, shops, landmarks, street furniture, park, waterfront,
port scenery, service locations and a cycle-based day/night presentation.

The delivery career contains six repeatable contracts, GPS guidance, road
markers, minimap, city map, delivery receipts, money, bonuses, XP, ranks,
milestones, customer history, fuel, repairs, workshop upgrades, roadside
assistance, pause/help/settings screens and local save/load. Career backups can
be exported and imported as JSON. Legacy local careers are migrated to the
DriveHorizon profile when saved.

## Engineering work already completed

- Career and UI integration, rank/milestone rewards and itemized receipts.
- Persistent tires, engine, efficiency and cargo-protection upgrades.
- Saved simulation time, independent fixed-rate physics and configurable
  render cadence/camera shake.
- Shared traffic signals, bounded AI following, moving-car impacts, paved
  service forecourts, GPS turn guidance, camera occlusion and traffic-safe
  resets.
- Save warnings, corrupt-save recovery, version-one migration, mission
  cancellation, new-career confirmation and session-only behavior when storage
  is unavailable.
- Explicit WebGL 2 preference with compatible WebGL 1 fallback, diagnostics,
  context-loss save/pause and restoration-to-pause behavior.
- Injectable non-GPU graphics boundary, game-loop tests, desktop protocol tests
  and production asset smoke checks.
- Local Electron host with sandboxed renderer, restricted asset protocol,
  fullscreen/quit bridge and save-on-close integration.

## Validation boundary

The environment-compatible checks use TypeScript, Node/JSDOM, synthetic canvas
and audio adapters, a non-rendering graphics adapter, Electron host adapters
and a real production HTTP server. They do not validate pixels, audio quality,
native-window behavior or hardware performance.

The current VMware guest reports `Accelerated: no` and lacks the WebGL feature
needed by the renderer. Browser screenshots, GPU context restoration and
benchmarks therefore require a capable physical or fully accelerated machine.
Do not treat historical screenshots as verification of a later build.
